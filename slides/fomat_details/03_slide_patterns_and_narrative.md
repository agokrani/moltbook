# Slide Patterns And Narrative

## Deck-level behavior

- 153 total slides
- 117 slides contain at least one image
- 9 slides contain native PowerPoint tables
- 0 slides contain native PowerPoint chart objects
- 56 slides have non-empty speaker notes
- 20 comment files are embedded in the deck

Interpretation:

- The deck is figure-first and chart-light.
- Quantitative visuals are usually brought in as rendered images rather than editable chart objects.
- Notes are part of the working format, not an afterthought.

## Common slide patterns

### 1. Cover or section opener

- Centered title
- Minimal body text
- Often tied to the alternate master/accent system

### 2. Title plus one dominant visual

- Most representative pattern in the deck
- One strong figure, screenshot, or diagram
- Small amount of supporting text

### 3. Title plus compact explanatory text

- Used for setup, framing, and takeaways
- Usually short paragraphs or a few simple bullets

### 4. Two-column compare slide

- Used when contrasting scenarios, personas, mechanisms, or outcomes
- Keeps the same top-title treatment, then splits the main content area

### 5. Sparse bridge or appendix slide

- Often near-blank or title-only
- Used to reset pacing, separate topics, or mark backup material

## Text density

Distribution of non-empty text shapes per slide is light by presentation standards:

- 30 slides have 2 text shapes
- 22 slides have 3 text shapes
- 22 slides have 4 text shapes
- 19 slides have 5 text shapes
- 14 slides have 6 text shapes
- 10 slides have only 1 text shape

Interpretation:

- The deck usually stays in the `1-5 text block` range.
- Slides are not built as dense outlines.

## Title conventions

Titles are descriptive and role-bearing. Common prefixes include:

- `Results`
- `Motivation`
- `Research Agenda`
- `Simulation`
- `Takeaway`

Practical rule:

- Write titles that say what the slide is doing, not just what topic it belongs to.
- Sentence-like titles fit this deck better than terse one-word headings.

## Notes, citations, and comments

- Speaker notes are used on 56 slides, with a median extracted length of roughly 171 characters.
- Comments are collaborative review artifacts from multiple contributors and should not be treated as part of the visual template itself.
- The deck keeps slides relatively clean and appears to push extra explanation into notes.

Practical rule:

- Put the long explanation in notes.
- Keep citations on-slide only when they can stay short and non-intrusive.

## Reusable authoring rules

- Start each major topic with a strong opener or title-only reset slide.
- Follow quickly with visual evidence: screenshots, plots, diagrams, or tables imported as assets.
- Use tables only when side-by-side comparison is the point.
- Prefer rendered figures over native PowerPoint charts.
- Keep slide bodies compact and let the title carry narrative framing.
