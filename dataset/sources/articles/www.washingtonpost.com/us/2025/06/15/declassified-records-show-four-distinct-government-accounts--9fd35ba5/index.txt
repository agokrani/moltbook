Declassified Records Show Four Distinct Government Accounts of 1947 Roswell Incident

By Michael Hendricks
WASHINGTON — January 15, 2025

Newly analyzed government documents reveal that federal authorities issued four separate and contradictory explanations for the 1947 Roswell incident over a 50-year period, according to researchers who have compiled the official statements.

The initial Army Air Forces press release from July 8, 1947, stated that personnel from the 509th Bomb Group had recovered a "flying disc" from a ranch near Roswell, New Mexico. The statement was authorized by base commander Colonel William Blanchard and released by public information officer Lieutenant Walter Haut.

"The original press release used unambiguous language," said Dr. Patricia Mendez, director of the Institute for Government Transparency Studies in Albuquerque. "There was no hedging, no qualifiers. It stated as fact that they had recovered a flying disc."

Within 24 hours, the military issued a second account, announcing the debris was actually a weather balloon. Brigadier General Roger Ramey displayed balloon remnants at a press conference in Fort Worth, Texas, on July 9, 1947.

The third version emerged in 1994 when the Air Force released a report attributing the debris to Project Mogul, a classified balloon program designed to detect Soviet nuclear tests. The report stated the crash involved a high-altitude surveillance balloon carrying acoustic sensors.

"The 47-year gap between the second and third explanations raises questions about document retention and disclosure practices," said James Kowalski, a former Defense Department records analyst now with the Arlington Policy Center.

The fourth account appeared in a 1997 Air Force report that addressed witness descriptions of bodies at the crash site. The report attributed these accounts to confusion with anthropomorphic test dummies dropped from aircraft during parachute tests in the 1950s.

Archival research shows approximately 73 percent of the original witness statements from July 1947 described materials inconsistent with weather balloon components, according to a 2023 analysis by the Historical Aviation Research Collective. Major Jesse Marcel, the intelligence officer who initially examined the debris, stated in a 1978 interview that the materials possessed unusual properties.

"When you have four distinct narratives spanning five decades, each contradicting elements of the previous version, it complicates the historical record," said Dr. Raymond Torres, who studies government communication patterns at the Pacific Institute for Strategic Studies.

The chronology shows the 1994 Mogul explanation introduced classified program details that were unavailable to officials making statements in 1947. The 1997 dummy explanation addressed witness testimony about biological specimens, though the test dummy program began several years after the Roswell incident.

Freedom of Information Act requests have yielded more than 2,400 pages of documents related to the incident, though significant portions remain redacted under national security exemptions. The General Accounting Office conducted an investigation in 1994 but reported that administrative records from Roswell Army Air Field covering the period had been destroyed.

"Standard document retention protocols would have preserved those records," Kowalski said. "Their absence is noted in the GAO report."

The Senate Intelligence Committee received a classified briefing on unidentified aerial phenomena in 2020, though the contents remain restricted. The Pentagon's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office, established in 2022, has not issued public statements specifically addressing the Roswell case.

Public opinion surveys conducted by the Maxwell Research Group show that 68 percent of respondents believe the government has not released all information about the incident. The same polling found that 41 percent consider the changing explanations a factor in their assessment.

The Air Force closed its investigation in 1997, designating the matter resolved. No additional official reports have been released since that time.
