Stock Photo Industry Sees $340M Windfall as Startups Default to Identical Branding

By Rachel Kowalski
SAN FRANCISCO — January 15, 2025

The technology startup ecosystem is fueling an unexpected boom in generic stock photography, with industry data showing that 78% of venture-backed companies launched in the past 18 months feature nearly identical visual branding on their corporate websites.

The trend has become so pronounced that stock image licensing revenues from the technology sector jumped 34% year-over-year to reach $340 million in 2024, according to figures compiled by Digital Asset Research Group, a Toronto-based analytics firm tracking visual content markets. The surge is driven almost entirely by a narrow selection of images depicting diverse teams gathered around laptops in deliberately casual office settings.

"We're seeing a convergence effect that would be remarkable in any other industry," said Dr. Marcus Chen, who studies corporate branding patterns at the Brennan Institute for Digital Commerce. "When you analyze the top 500 seed-stage company websites, the visual and textual homogeneity is statistically significant. We're talking about a 92% overlap in design elements."

The phenomenon extends beyond imagery. Chen's research team identified that gradient backgrounds transitioning from purple to blue appear on 64% of technology startup homepages, while the term "reimagine" features in headlines at a rate 11 times higher than comparable corporate sites in traditional industries. The word "Get Started" dominates call-to-action buttons with 71% market share, compared to just 8% for the next most common phrase.

For stock photography platforms, the standardization represents a significant revenue opportunity. Shares of Shutterstock Inc. (SSTK) have climbed 23% over the past six months, outperforming the broader market, with analysts attributing part of the gain to sustained enterprise licensing from the startup sector. The company declined to comment on customer-specific trends.

Venture capital firms are taking notice of the branding uniformity, though reactions vary. "It's actually become a negative signal during due diligence," said Jennifer Oakes, a partner at Westbridge Capital Partners, a mid-market venture firm managing $890 million in assets. "When a founding team hasn't invested any creative energy in their own presentation, it raises questions about their ability to differentiate in their actual market."

The convergence appears to stem from a handful of website template providers that dominate the startup ecosystem. Webflow, Squarespace, and similar platforms offer pre-designed sections that companies can deploy with minimal customization. Data from web technology tracker BuiltWith shows that 82% of Series A companies use one of three template frameworks, up from 41% in 2019.

Branding consultancies report that startups are increasingly willing to pay premiums to escape the visual monotony. "We've had a 60% increase in inbound requests specifically mentioning the need to 'not look like everyone else,'" said Thomas Brennan, creative director at Meridian Brand Studio. His firm now charges between $45,000 and $120,000 for custom startup website design, compared to $15,000 three years ago.

The issue has practical implications for customer acquisition costs. A/B testing data from conversion optimization firm Quartile Analytics indicates that startups using standard stock photography see 18% lower click-through rates on call-to-action buttons compared to those with custom imagery, translating to higher customer acquisition expenses.

Some founders defend the approach as efficient resource allocation. "At the seed stage, you're spending money on product and distribution, not photography," noted one founder who requested anonymity. However, marketing analysts suggest the long-term brand equity costs may outweigh the short-term savings.

The stock photography sector continues to benefit regardless. Industry forecasts project technology sector licensing revenue will reach $425 million by 2026, assuming current adoption patterns hold steady.
