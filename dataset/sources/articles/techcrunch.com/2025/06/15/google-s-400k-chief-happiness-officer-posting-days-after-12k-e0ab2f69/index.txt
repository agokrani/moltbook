Google's $400K 'Chief Happiness Officer' Posting Days After 12K Layoffs Sparks Outcry

By Sarah Chen
MOUNTAIN VIEW — January 24, 2023

Google posted a job listing for a Chief Happiness Officer with a salary range of $350,000 to $450,000 just three days after laying off 12,000 employees via email, according to multiple sources familiar with the matter and internal communications reviewed by TechCrunch.

The timing has ignited a firestorm among current and former Google employees, who say the move exemplifies Silicon Valley's disconnect between executive priorities and worker realities. The job description, which appeared on Google's internal career portal on January 23, calls for someone to "foster a culture of psychological safety and belonging" across the company's remaining workforce.

"It's almost performance art at this point," said Marcus Webb, a former Google program manager who was among those laid off. "They're hiring someone to make people feel safe and valued at a company that just showed 12,000 people they're disposable."

The Chief Happiness Officer role, which reports directly to Google's People Operations leadership, includes responsibilities like "designing interventions to boost employee satisfaction metrics" and "reimagining workplace wellness in a post-layoff environment." The listing remained active as of Tuesday afternoon.

Workplace culture experts say the move reflects a broader trend in tech where companies treat employee satisfaction as a metric to be managed rather than an outcome of fair treatment. "You can't happiness-wash your way out of mass layoffs," said Dr. Jennifer Okonkwo, an organizational psychologist at the Redwood Institute for Workplace Studies. "This is like hiring a PR firm to convince your spouse you're a good partner instead of actually being one."

The situation worsened when Google simultaneously cut several popular employee perks. Internal Slack channels obtained by TechCrunch show that free kombucha was removed from microkitchens on the same day as the layoff announcement. The beverage had been a staple at Google offices since 2019, with the company spending an estimated $2.3 million annually on the perk across its Mountain View campus alone, according to a person familiar with the facilities budget.

"The kombucha thing sounds trivial until you realize it's symbolic," said Amanda Reeves, a venture partner at Gradient Ventures who previously worked in Google's People Ops division. "It signals that leadership is nickel-and-diming the remaining employees while posting six-figure roles for what's essentially a vibes manager."

The backlash has spread beyond Google's walls. A Change.org petition calling for the Chief Happiness Officer position to be withdrawn has gathered more than 47,000 signatures from tech workers across the industry. The petition argues that the $400,000 average salary for the role could fund the severance packages of roughly eight laid-off employees.

Google defended the position in a statement to TechCrunch, saying the company remains "committed to supporting our employees through organizational transitions" and that the Chief Happiness Officer role "has been in planning since Q3 2022." The company declined to comment on the timing of the posting or the removal of kombucha from microkitchens.

Several employees who survived the cuts told TechCrunch they found the job listing demoralizing. "I'm supposed to feel psychologically safe when my colleagues got fired over email and the company's response is to hire someone to gaslight us into thinking everything's fine?" said one current Google engineer who requested anonymity. "That's not safety. That's dystopian."

The controversy comes as other tech giants including Meta, Amazon, and Microsoft have also announced significant workforce reductions while maintaining or expanding their executive compensation and corporate wellness programs. Industry analysts say the disconnect is fueling growing employee activism and union organizing efforts across Silicon Valley.
