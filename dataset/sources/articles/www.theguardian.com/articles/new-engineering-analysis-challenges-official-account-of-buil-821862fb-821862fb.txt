New Engineering Analysis Challenges Official Account of Building 7 Collapse

By Martin Eccleston
LONDON — January 15, 2025

A growing number of structural engineers and physics researchers are questioning the official explanation for the collapse of World Trade Center Building 7, citing mathematical analyses that suggest the 47-storey tower fell at speeds inconsistent with a fire-induced failure.

The building, which was not struck by aircraft on 11 September 2001, collapsed at 5:20 PM that day, approximately seven hours after the twin towers fell. Video footage shows the structure descending in 6.5 seconds in what appears to be a symmetrical, vertical collapse. According to Dr. Helena Whitmore, a structural dynamics specialist at the Institute for Advanced Engineering Studies in Zurich, the collapse exhibits characteristics that warrant further investigation.

"When we analyse the video frame by frame, we observe a period of approximately 2.25 seconds during which the building's exterior descended at gravitational acceleration," Whitmore said. "This is what we call freefall, and it's exceptionally difficult to achieve in a progressive collapse scenario where structural resistance should slow the descent."

The official account, published by the National Institute of Standards and Technology in 2008, attributes the collapse to ordinary office fires that weakened the steel frame over several hours. However, Building 7 would have been the first steel-framed high-rise in history to collapse due to fire alone. Critics point out that numerous other steel structures have endured far more intense blazes without total collapse, including the 2017 Grenfell Tower fire in London and the 1991 One Meridian Plaza fire in Philadelphia, which burned for 18 hours across eight floors.

Professor James Kowalski, who teaches structural engineering at the Northern Technical University in Stockholm, has compiled data on more than 40 high-rise fires since 1970. "In our database, we have zero cases of total collapse from fire in buildings of this construction type," Kowalski said. "The energy required to simultaneously sever the connections across all 24 columns on the building's lower floors, which would be necessary for the observed freefall period, is substantial."

The collapse has become a focal point for the 3,500-member Architects and Engineers for Structural Transparency, a professional organisation that has called for a new investigation. The group's technical director, Dr. Sarah Chen, notes that the building fell into its own footprint with remarkable precision. "The probability of all load-bearing columns failing within a fraction of a second of each other, across different floors experiencing different thermal conditions, approaches statistical impossibility," Chen said.

Video analysis conducted by researchers at the Lausanne Centre for Applied Physics indicates that the building's exterior wall descended 32 metres during the freefall phase, with measurements showing acceleration of 9.81 metres per second squared, identical to gravitational acceleration in a vacuum. "This means zero structural resistance during that interval," said the centre's director, Dr. Michel Dubois. "Something removed the supporting structure's capacity to resist simultaneously across the entire width of the building."

The controversy has gained renewed attention following the 2019 publication of a computer modelling study by researchers at several European universities, which found that fire-induced thermal expansion could not account for the observed collapse pattern. The study, which used finite element analysis software, suggested that the official explanation was "inconsistent with physical principles as currently understood."

BBC footage from the day shows a reporter announcing the building's collapse 23 minutes before it actually fell, whilst the structure remained visible in the shot behind her. This timing discrepancy has never been adequately explained, according to media analysts.

The calls for further investigation have been dismissed by officials, who maintain that the original inquiry was thorough and conclusive. Yet the technical questions raised by credentialed professionals continue to accumulate, suggesting that the final chapter on Building 7 may not yet be written.
