A post is being shared on social media (here, here, and here) claiming that, under a new order issued by the Assam government, the buying and selling of land between people of different religions is no longer permitted in the state. The post further alleges that Muslims are prohibited from purchasing land from Hindus, suggesting that the Assam government has issued an order banning such transactions. Let’s verify the claim made in the post in this article.
Claim: A new order by the Assam government does not allow people of different religions to buy or sell land to each other.
Fact: The Assam government has not banned land transactions between people of different religions. A new Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) has streamlined the process and made verification mandatory to prevent fraud, coercion, or social tension. Inter-religion land sales are still allowed. Hence, the claim made in the post is MISLEADING.
To verify the claim, we searched for official notifications and credible media reports but found no evidence that the Assam government has banned land transactions between people of different religions. Instead, multiple reports (here, here, and here) state that the government has approved a Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) to regulate inter-religious land transfers. Under this SOP, the procedure for granting permission has been streamlined to ensure efficient processing and quicker disposal of such proposals. Police permission has been made mandatory for buying, selling, or transferring land between people of different religions. All applications for such land transfers must be submitted to the government and routed through the District Commissioner, who forwards them to the Revenue Department. The aim is to examine whether the transaction is voluntary and whether it could disturb social harmony in the area.
Meanwhile, Assam Chief Minister Himanta Biswa Sarma stated that the SOP was approved because land transfers between different religious groups in a sensitive state like Assam must be handled “very, very carefully.” He explained that a designated Nodal Officer in the Revenue Department will send the proposal to the Special Branch of the Assam Police, which will scrutinise each case to identify any elements of fraud, coercion, or illegality, verify the source of funds and whether they are reflected in tax returns, assess the impact on social cohesion in the locality, consider objections from residents, and examine any national security concerns. After this scrutiny, the proposal is returned to the government, and the District Commissioner makes the final decision on whether to approve or reject the transfer. He also clarified that the same procedure applies to NGOs from outside Assam seeking land for educational or health institutions, while recognised local NGOs are exempt. At no point in his briefing did the Chief Minister state that the Assam government has prohibited land transactions between people of different religions.
The official Assam Cabinet decision dated 27 August 2025 further clarifies the SOP for inter-religion land transfers. The Cabinet approved streamlining the procedure for granting permission for such transfers to prevent fraudulent, coercive, or illegal land deals that could create communal tension. The SOP also ensures compliance with constitutional principles of equality and non-discrimination, reinforcing that the process is regulatory and protective, not prohibitive.
To sum up, the Assam government has not banned land sales between different religions; it has only made verification mandatory.
